A Graph-Theoretic Clustering Approach to Social Network Analysis

OBJECTIVE

To model a professional social network as a graph and apply community detection techniques to uncover meaningful clusters of closely connected individuals.

DATASET

148 anonymized professional profiles.

METHODOLOGY
  • Modeled the professional profiles as a network graph
  • Computed structural metrics: clustering coefficient and network diameter
  • Applied the Louvain algorithm for community detection
  • Applied the Girvan–Newman algorithm for comparison across hierarchical levels
  • Visualized resulting communities using Matplotlib and NetworkX
ALGORITHMS

Louvain (modularity optimization) and Girvan–Newman (edge-betweenness based divisive clustering) were used to detect community structure and compare the results.

RESULTS
  • Louvain algorithm identified 6 distinct communities with a modularity score of 0.508
  • Average clustering coefficient of 0.4
  • Network diameter of 7
  • Girvan–Newman's best-performing level produced 5 communities
KEY FINDINGS

Louvain detected 6 communities (modularity 0.508); the best Girvan–Newman level produced 5. Average clustering coefficient: 0.4. Network diameter: 7.
